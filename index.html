ScreenWatch continuously monitors a region you pick — a button, a counter, a progress bar — and clicks a point you pick the moment it changes visually. Selection happens live, directly on your real screen. Built to sit in the background for hours on almost nothing.

What it does

Live, real-screen selection

The screen is never covered while you select. Drag over your real, moving desktop to pick the watch area and the click point — no frozen snapshot.

Colour-aware detection

Reacts to hue changes, not just brightness — a button swapping colour is caught even when a plain grayscale check would miss it. A noise filter ignores render/compression jitter.

Two detection modes

React to any change from the previous frame, or to a deviation from a fixed starting state — whichever matches what you're watching for.

See exactly why it clicked

Every click is logged with a picture of the pixels that triggered it — changed areas highlighted in red against a desaturated background, browsable per click.

Customizable hotkeys

Start, stop, and quit with global keyboard shortcuts you record yourself, matched robustly across keyboard layouts and modifier states.

Built for long runs

The watched region is down-sampled before every comparison, so detection cost stays independent of your monitor size — comfortably under 1% of one CPU core for a multi-hour session.

How it works

  1. Select the region to watch, right on your live screen.
  2. Select the point to click when it changes.
  3. Tune sensitivity — react to a flicker, or only to something big.
  4. Press Start. ScreenWatch clicks the instant the region changes, and logs why.
